# Arabic Dialect Evaluation

Dialect quality tested with dialect-specific conversational sentences, never with MSA text
(per the project brief). Full sample text and expected behavior for every case, including
these, is in [ARABIC_TEST_CASES.md](ARABIC_TEST_CASES.md).

## Test sentences used

**Levantine (Lebanese)**:
> كيفك؟ شو الأخبار؟ اليوم الجو كتير حلو وعم فكّر نطلع نشرب قهوة.

**Gulf (Saudi)**:
> شلونك؟ إن شاء الله أمورك طيبة، وش رايك نطلع نتعشى اليوم؟

## What "dialect support" actually means per provider — the required distinction

The brief requires distinguishing three different things a provider might mean by "supports
a dialect." This project makes that distinction explicit rather than collapsing it:

| Level | Meaning | Who this project claims it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Official provider support** | Vendor publishes a distinct locale/model trained for that dialect | Groq (`orpheus-arabic-saudi` — Saudi/Gulf), Edge/Microsoft (16 locales, but see caveat below) |
| **Voice appears capable of speaking it** | No dialect-specific training documented; a voice may render dialect *text* intelligibly through general multilingual capability | ElevenLabs (Arabic text is accepted; no dialect locale is published) |
| **Multilingual model, no dialect guarantee** | Arabic is one of many auto-detected languages; no Arabic-specific training claimed at all | OpenAI, Gemini |

## The caveat that matters most: Edge's 16 locales are not what Groq's 1 dialect is

This is the single most important finding of this evaluation, and it is easy to miss by
just counting locale codes:

- **Edge/Microsoft's 16 Arabic locales are neural voices in the same underlying model
  family, trained predominantly on Modern Standard Arabic pronunciation**, each locale-coded
  for regional accent conventions rather than retrained on colloquial dialect speech. This
  project's own catalogue reflects that: every Edge/Microsoft voice, including `ar-SA`,
  carries `dialect=MSA` or a family label used for **locale-based routing**, not a claim of
  colloquial authenticity (per FR-027 — a family is claimed only where defensible).
- **Groq's `orpheus-arabic-saudi` is documented as trained specifically on colloquial
  Saudi/Gulf dialect speech** — a genuinely different training objective, not merely a
  different locale tag. This project's catalogue reflects that too: Groq's six voices carry
  `dialect=GULF`, sharing the `ar-SA` locale string with Edge's MSA-trained `ar-SA` voices
  but asserting a materially different claim about what comes out of the model.

So "16 locales vs. 1" understates Groq's contribution and overstates Edge's: Edge offers
**broad locale routing on an MSA-trained voice family**; Groq offers **narrow but genuine
dialect training** on exactly the dialect (Saudi/Gulf) this evaluation's own Gulf test
sentence targets. Neither claim substitutes for the other — routing breadth and dialect
authenticity are different axes, and this project's `Capabilities`/`VoiceConfig` models keep
them as separate fields (`locales` vs. `dialect`) specifically so this distinction survives
into the running code, not just this document.

## Per-provider assessment

### Edge / Microsoft neural voices (16 locales, MSA-trained)

- **Accent authenticity**: Not independently perceptually verified against native-speaker
  judgment in this environment (no ASR/human-rating tool available — Constitution V forbids
  claiming this was confirmed when it was not; see the same disclosure pattern in
  `PRONUNCIATION.md`). The dialect *sentences* above are synthesizable (the pipeline sends
  them through unmodified — dialect text is never rewritten toward MSA, per FR-013/FR-014)
  but the resulting *accent* is not asserted to sound authentically Lebanese or Saudi.
- **Vocabulary pronunciation**: Colloquial words in the test sentences (كتير, شو, وش, شلونك)
  are passed through the pipeline unchanged — no dialect-to-MSA normalization occurs.
  Whether the underlying MSA-trained model renders them with authentic dialect prosody is
  the open, disclosed question above.
- **English code-switching**: Handled by the shared neural model; not independently
  dialect-scored (would require a native-speaker rating panel, out of scope for this
  prototype per Constitution VIII — testability without disproportionate infrastructure).
- **MSA drift risk**: Elevated, precisely because the voices are MSA-trained — this is the
  documented, expected behavior of this adapter for dialect text, not a defect discovered
  in testing.

### Groq — Orpheus Arabic (Saudi dialect-trained)

- **Accent authenticity**: Vendor-documented as trained on authentic Saudi dialect speech.
  `GROQ_API_KEY` was later configured and live-verified (see `docs/BENCHMARK_RESULTS.md`) —
  real audio now exists (`docs/audio/dialect/gulf-*-groq.mp3`, generated by
  `scripts/observe_dialect_tts.py`), but *dialect-accuracy* itself (does it actually sound
  Saudi to a native speaker) still has not been perceptually scored by a human listener or
  an ASR/dialect-classifier tool in this environment — that remains the vendor's claim,
  correctly labeled as such, not independently confirmed. This is disclosed here rather than
  silently upgraded to a verified claim just because the audio now exists.
- **MSA drift risk**: Documented as low by design — the model's whole training objective is
  colloquial Saudi/Gulf speech, not MSA with a Gulf accent layered on top.
- **English code-switching**: Not documented by the vendor and not independently tested
  (200-char-per-call limit makes long mixed-language sentences require this project's
  chunking logic — `_chunk_text` in `groq.py` — which itself is unit-tested for
  word-boundary safety, but the *audio* result for code-switched text was not perceptually
  evaluated without a live key).

### ElevenLabs (no documented Arabic locale)

- No dialect locale is published, so per FR-027 this project's catalogue assigns
  `dialect=None` to its Arabic voices rather than inferring Lebanese/Gulf/etc. from a voice
  name or marketing description — asserting one would be exactly the unverified claim
  Constitution V forbids.
- Both test sentences above are synthesizable through it (multilingual model accepts
  Arabic text unmodified), but with no basis to claim which, if any, dialect the output
  resembles.

### OpenAI / Gemini (no Arabic dialect claim at all)

- Both accept the dialect test sentences as plain Arabic input (general multilingual
  capability) but publish no Arabic dialect information whatsoever — not evaluated further
  for dialect authenticity, since there is no vendor claim to test against, and inventing an
  expectation to then measure would itself violate Constitution V.

## Bottom line (pre-Hugging Face)

No single provider in this evaluation offers *both* broad dialect-locale routing *and*
verified colloquial-dialect training. This project's dialect router (`voice_router.py`)
reflects that reality rather than papering over it: a dialect request resolves to whichever
voice a provider actually publishes for that family, with the routing decision — and which
kind of "dialect support" backs it — inspectable in the response rather than asserted
uniformly across providers.

## Hugging Face dialect/pronunciation experimentation results (US6-7, FR-054, FR-062)

Full model research and licensing/relevance decisions are in
[HF_MODEL_RESEARCH.md](HF_MODEL_RESEARCH.md) and `specs/001-arabic-tts-prototype/research.md`
R10-R14. This section answers FR-062 directly, per dialect, from what was actually run.

### What was actually run

`scripts/observe_dialect_tts.py` generated real audio for every dialect's sample sentences
(spec.md's Lebanese, Gulf, and Egyptian conversational sets) through every architecture that
was actually reachable in this environment, saved under `docs/audio/dialect/`:

| Dialect | Existing TTS baseline(s) run | Hugging Face candidate run? |
|---|---|---|
| Lebanese / Levantine | Edge `ar-LB` (5/5 samples, real audio) | **No** — no HF dialect-specific TTS candidate cleared research.md R11's license/relevance check for this dialect |
| Saudi / Gulf | Edge `ar-SA` MSA baseline + **Groq Orpheus Arabic** (4/4 samples each, real audio, live `GROQ_API_KEY`) | **No** — same as above |
| Egyptian | Edge `ar-EG` (3/3 samples, real audio) | **Attempted live, confirmed blocked by the platform, not by credentials** — see below |

### Update (2026-09-07, later the same day): HF_TOKEN configured, live probes completed

A real `HF_TOKEN` was configured and granted the `inference.serverless.write` permission
(the Hugging Face equivalent of the earlier ElevenLabs `voices_read` fix — a valid token
initially scoped without it, confirmed via `GET /api/whoami-v2`, 403 → fixed → retested).
With permissions confirmed correct, both enabled Hugging Face models were called live and
**both are genuinely not servable through Hugging Face's current Inference Providers
system**, independent of any token or code issue:

- **`oddadmix/chatterbox-egyptian-v0`** (dialect TTS): `400 Bad Request` — *"Model not
  supported by provider hf-inference"*. The model's own metadata
  (`GET huggingface.co/api/models/oddadmix/chatterbox-egyptian-v0`) shows `"inference": null`
  — no Inference Provider has deployed it.
- **`IbrahimAmin/marbertv2-arabic-written-dialect-classifier`** (dialect detection):
  `410 Gone` — *"the requested model is deprecated and no longer supported by provider
  hf-inference"*.

A real, separate bug was also found and fixed along the way: `text_processing/huggingface/`'s
raw `httpx` calls (classifier, diacritizer, G2P) were still pointed at
`api-inference.huggingface.co`, Hugging Face's **retired** legacy free-tier hostname (DNS
no longer resolves it at all) — fixed to `router.huggingface.co/hf-inference/models`, the
current Inference Providers gateway. This fix is real and independent of the two findings
above: it is what let those findings surface as clean, mappable HTTP errors (400/410)
instead of an opaque `ConnectError`.

**What this means, concretely**: the hosted-Inference-API-first default (research.md R12)
remains sound as an architecture, but it only works for models an Inference Provider has
actually deployed — a real constraint niche, community-maintained fine-tunes frequently
don't clear, independent of licensing. Both integration tests
(`test_huggingface_live_synthesis_when_configured`,
`test_huggingface_dialect_classifier_live_when_configured`) now skip with this exact,
confirmed reason rather than fail — the same "skip what genuinely can't run here, fail what's
actually broken" principle already applied to ElevenLabs' `payment_required` case.

### FR-062 answers, stated honestly

- **Did Hugging Face measurably improve dialect authenticity for any dialect?** **No —
  and now for a confirmed platform reason, not a missing-credential one.** The one eligible
  HF dialect-specific TTS candidate (Egyptian) cannot be called at all through Hugging
  Face's current Inference Providers system (400, model not deployed by any provider);
  Lebanese/Levantine and Saudi/Gulf have no cleared candidate to begin with. A measurable
  "did it help" answer would require either a Dedicated Inference Endpoint (paid) or local
  execution for this specific model — both a materially bigger lift than "add a token."
- **Did Hugging Face measurably improve pronunciation?** **Partially assessable, same as
  before.** The pronunciation-dictionary layer (`data/pronunciation_dictionary.py`, FR-052)
  is live, offline, and demonstrated working — real, deterministic corrections (e.g. بيروت
  → بَيْرُوت). The HF diacritizer/G2P candidates are disabled pending license confirmation
  (unaffected by this update, since they were never reached); the HF dialect
  *classifier* that would drive automatic detection is now confirmed **also** blocked by
  the same 410 "deprecated, not served by hf-inference" finding as the TTS candidate.
- **Which HF model worked best for Lebanese/Levantine?** None — no candidate cleared R11.
- **Which HF model worked best for Saudi/Gulf?** None — no candidate cleared R11; **Groq's
  Orpheus Arabic** (an existing, non-HF provider) remains this evaluation's best-evidenced
  dialect-trained option for this family.
- **Which HF model worked best for Egyptian?** None — `oddadmix/chatterbox-egyptian-v0` is
  confirmed, live, not callable through the hosted path. Existing Edge `ar-EG` remains the
  only actually-producing option for this dialect today.
- **Which worked best for MSA?** Not applicable — the only HF MSA candidate found
  (`facebook/mms-tts-ara`) is excluded by its non-commercial license, unrelated to this
  finding.
- **Should Hugging Face generate the final audio, or preprocess for another provider?**
  **Preprocess, and now more confidently.** Every eligible dialect-specific *TTS* path is
  confirmed unreachable through the free hosted API; the *preprocessing* capabilities
  (dialect classification via text, the pronunciation dictionary) remain the only
  demonstrated-working Hugging Face contribution, and even the classifier now needs a
  different model or execution mode to actually run.
- **What is the best production architecture?** Unchanged in shape, strengthened in
  evidence: see [PRODUCTION_ARCHITECTURE.md](PRODUCTION_ARCHITECTURE.md). For a production
  deployment intending to actually use `chatterbox-egyptian-v0` or the marbertv2
  classifier, the real next step is a **Hugging Face Dedicated Inference Endpoint** (which
  deploys the model regardless of whether a shared provider has picked it up) or **local
  execution** — not simply enabling `HF_TOKEN`, which this update proves is not sufficient
  on its own for these two specific models.

### Honesty note on accent/prosody scoring

No ASR or native-speaker rating tool was available in this environment (same limitation
already disclosed for the pre-Hugging-Face evaluation above and in `PRONUNCIATION.md`) — the
FR-061 listening rubric (naturalness, pronunciation, dialect accuracy, prosody,
English-mixing, voice quality, each /5) has **not** been scored against the generated audio
files. They are real, playable, and available at `docs/audio/dialect/` for a human listener
to score; this document will be updated with actual numbers once that scoring happens,
rather than inventing plausible-looking scores now.
